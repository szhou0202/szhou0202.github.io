Humans, agents, and learning

10/26/26

I've been working on a side project to build a personally-hosted personal assistant. It's very primitive so far. I originally endowed it with 2 abilities: setting reminders on a custom schedule, and internet research. Every day, I've been monitoring the agent's performance and prompt-tuning it so that it behaves better.

I've also been trying to up-level myself -- by reading things, trying new things, phasing in new habits, and phasing out old ones. Tonight I noticed that gaining information is always well and good, but after that, there remains the question: what learning algorithm do I now enact to get me from point A to point B, point "me now" to point "a better me"? How do humans efficiently search for the right solution; in fact, how do humans efficiently search for the right search algorithm in the first place? If we knew, could we put that into mathematical and computational terms?

This has all made me wonder: am I not just doing in-context learning on myself? I am giving myself a lot of context (books and podcasts) and prompts ("do ___ n times a week") and applying the behaviors and logic that I have now to acquire new ones. Maybe agents have much more similar thoughts, thought-making, and learning algorithms to us than I thought.
